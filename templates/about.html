Methodology & System Architecture

Understanding the optical inspection pipeline, defect classification, and mathematical scoring model.

1. What is OnionVision AI?

OnionVision AI is an automated, computer-vision-powered prototype built to provide fast, objective, preliminary visual quality assessment of post-harvest onions (*Allium cepa*).

In traditional agricultural supply chains (mandi procurement centers, cold storages, and packhouses), grading is largely performed manually. This approach is prone to visual fatigue, subjective disputes between growers and commission agents, inconsistent grading standards, and delayed sorting. OnionVision AI provides an accessible digital inspection layer to assist sorters, standardize preliminary grading, and generate verifiable audit reports.

2. End-to-End Processing Pipeline

Stage 1: Image Acquisition
Accepts high-resolution lot captures via digital upload or browser-based camera feed.
Stage 2: OpenCV Preprocessing
Edge-preserving Non-Local Means denoising, automatic brightness normalization, and aspect-ratio scaling.
Stage 3: Object Detection
Ultralytics YOLO single-stage detector segments individual bulb bounding boxes and locations.
Stage 4: Defect Classification
Categorizes each bulb into one of 5 target quality classes with confidence probability.
Stage 5: Percentage Calculation
Computes the proportion of healthy versus defective bulbs across the sample lot.
Stage 6: Weighted Quality Scoring
Applies the transparent mathematical scoring formula to compute an overall 0–100 index.
Stage 7: Preliminary Grading
Maps the quality index into commercial market grades (Grade A, B, C, or URS).
Stage 8: Digital Certificate Generation
Compiles metadata, visual evidence, metrics, and disclaimers into a downloadable PDF certificate.

3. Classification Taxonomy & Scoring Weights

Class Standard Points Visual Indicators Commercial Significance
Good 100 pts Intact dry papery tunic, firm bulb, clean root plate, no mold or cuts. Premium commercial / export quality.
Damaged 50 pts Mechanical bruising, harvest gouges, peeled outer layers, cuts. Acceptable for immediate domestic consumption; poor storage life.
Rotten 0 pts Black/blue mold (*Aspergillus niger*), soft wet rot, foul decomposition. Severe defect. Rejected from consumption. Spoilage risk to neighboring bulbs.
Sprouted 40 pts Emergent green shoots breaking out from the onion neck. Indicates broken dormancy. Rapid loss of internal moisture and pungency.
Undersized 60 pts Bulb equatorial diameter < 40mm (sub-caliber small onions). Lower market value; relegated to processing or low-grade distribution.
Mathematical Scoring Equation
Score = (Good × 100 + Damaged × 50 + Sprouted × 40 + Undersized × 60 + Rotten × 0) / Total

Each detected bulb contributes to the lot's overall index proportional to its commercial viability. Rotten bulbs contribute 0 points, drastically penalizing high-spoilage batches.

Preliminary Grade Tiers
  • Grade A (Score ≥ 90):
    Premium Export Quality – Minimal to zero defects.
    90–100
  • Grade B (Score 75–89):
    Good Domestic Market Quality – Minor acceptable defects.
    75–89
  • Grade C (Score 60–74):
    Fair / Processing Grade – Significant sorting required.
    60–74
  • URS (Score < 60):
    Under Review / Substandard – High spoilage and rejection risk.
    < 60
Important Operational Limitations & Disclaimers

Model Dependencies: The optical classification performance depends heavily on physical capture conditions:

  • Lighting: Uneven or shadowed lighting may disguise discoloration or mimic rotten spots.
  • Orientation: Defects located on the underside of a resting bulb cannot be detected from a single top-down image.
  • Overlapping: Closely piled onions can cause partial occlusion or inaccurate bounding box grouping.
Official Legal Notice: OnionVision AI is a preliminary computer-vision screening tool designed for educational, research, and assistive sorting workflows. It does not substitute for official government statutory certification (such as AGMARK, APEDA, or FSSAI). Commercial settlements must involve manual inspection.
